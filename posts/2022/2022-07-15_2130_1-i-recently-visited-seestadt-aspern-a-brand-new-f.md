## 1/10

1/ I recently visited Seestadt Aspern, a brand new family friendly high-density suburb of Vienna. As a North-American, this place felt surreal, for my whole life i've been told that raising kids could only occur in low density car dependent hell-scapes. (brief thread)

![[1548057540457402370-FXvOHs1UIAEbMBt.jpg]]

![[1548057540457402370-FXvOHsmUsAAg9z9.jpg]]

## 2/10

2/ Built on the land of Austria's largest former airport, Seestadt is one of the biggest and most ambitious urban development projects in Europe. It has a planned gross floor space of 2.6 million square metres with 25k planned residents. Costing €5 billion to build.

![[1548057566550274051-FXvOInOUUAAh4eL.jpg]]

![[1548057566550274051-FXvOIqOVQAMBrwb.jpg]]

*❤ 189 · 🔁 8*

## 3/10

3/ According to the master plan, the envisaged modal split for local traffic is 40% cycling and walking, 40% public transport and just 20%  motorised traffic. I could definitely tell by the number of car-free or car-light streets.

## 4/10

4/ Because of the higher density, parents feel safe leaving their kids to explore the suburb independently. There's always other kids and  parents outside (safety in numbers), and obviously there's little danger of being run over by cars given the walkable street design.

## 5/10

5/ There's multiple car-free plazas that include grocery stores, restaurants, shops and day care centers.

![[1548057746796187653-FXvOTZSVEAESkWY.jpg]]

## 6/10

6/ Importantly, Seestadt features of mix of housing type to cater different levels of affordability. It includes private market housing, partially subsidised housing, full social housing, student housing, & co-op housing.

*❤ 147 · 🔁 6*

## 7/10

7/ For subsidised rental housing, developers must take part in a competition where a jury of architects, planners, ecologists and sociologists evaluate each project. Ensuring that each development is sustainably integrated within the suburb.
