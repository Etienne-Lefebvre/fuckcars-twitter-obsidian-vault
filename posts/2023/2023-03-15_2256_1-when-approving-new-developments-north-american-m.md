## 1/11

1/ When approving new developments, North American municipalities delegate significant political power to residents and community associations. This public engagement process is said to be democratic. In reality, the opposite is true. 🧵

*❤ 148 · 🔁 16*

## 2/11

2/ Every time a street is redesigned, a development is proposed, or a new infrastructure project is announced, there's always an opportunity for the public to have their say via town halls, public engagement meetings, committee testimony sessions, etc...

*❤ 46 · 🔁 0*

## 3/11

3/ Generally, the only people who show up to public engagement meetings are those with lots of wealth and free time.

*❤ 55 · 🔁 1*

## 4/11

4/ This means you end up with a political system where you place a disproportionate amount of power into the hands of wealthy property-owning residents who have a financial interest in lobbying the city in a manner that protects their property values.

*❤ 57 · 🔁 1*

## 5/11

5/ The consequence of such a system is that city council members are less able to implement the policy agenda they campaigned on during the elections.

*❤ 43 · 🔁 1*

## 6/11

6/ Constantly engaging the "public" before any project is greenlit means that NIMBY groups will have plenty of opportunities to delay projects by asking for more feasibility studies, engagement sessions, city surveys, etc...

*❤ 47 · 🔁 1*

## 7/11

7/ The result is an erosion of public trust in the democratic system, taking the form of constant gridlock, a sense that the rich control the political system, lower voter turnout, and a near impossibility of enacting popular projects in a timely fashion.

*❤ 40 · 🔁 0*

## 8/11

8/ The speed at which cities like Paris are transforming their cities away from car dependency is made possible by restricting the degree to which wealthy property owners can influence major infrastructure developments.

*❤ 46 · 🔁 3*

## 9/11

9/ When a majority elects candidates who ran on a pro-urbanist, pro-housing, pro-climate adaptive platform, it's undemocratic to delay and impede their legislative authority by mandating public engagement sessions (attended by a tiny minority of wealthy homeowners).

*❤ 54 · 🔁 1*

## 10/11

10/ More and more people want to live in walkable/accessible mixed-use communities featuring climate-adaptive and affordable housing. To get there quickly, we must rethink who gets prioritized in the political process. We need more real democracy, not more "public" engagement.
