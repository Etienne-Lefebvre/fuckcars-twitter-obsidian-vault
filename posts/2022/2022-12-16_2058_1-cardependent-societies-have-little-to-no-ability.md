## 7/11

7/ Public transit systems have relatively little upkeep/operational costs compared to car infrastructure, meaning they are much better suited to effectively provide transportation services during a financial downturn.

*❤ 29 · 🔁 3*

## 8/11

8/ The same is true with active transpo infrastructure. Building cities more densely means that more people are able to walk or cycle to meet their daily transport needs. Overall this reduces the financial burden on roads and public transit, making the system even more resilient.

*❤ 28 · 🔁 2*

## 9/11

9/ It's important to understand how financially harmful car-dependency truly is. Defaulting on your loans has massive negative repercussions that can drastically change someone's life for the worse. It doesn't have to be this way, we can change this pretty easily.

*❤ 33 · 🔁 2*

## 10/11

10/ We also need to avoid framing solutions in terms of individual choices. Sure people could have bought a cheap used car instead of leasing a new car, but this framing doesn't speak to the core issue of what led people to only consider using cars in the first place.

*❤ 42 · 🔁 2*

## 11/11

11/ The truth is that if we want a financially resilient transportation system it means lowering the total costs of the system as a whole. And the only way to do this is to push for policies that reduce the amount of expensive transpo infrastructure which = ending car-dependency.
