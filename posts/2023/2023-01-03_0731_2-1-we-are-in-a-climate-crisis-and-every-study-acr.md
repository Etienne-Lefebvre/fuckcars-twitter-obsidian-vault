## 5/19

6/ Before we get to policy, it's important to understand why cities currently have so much sprawl and car-dependency. Theres dozens of reasons, but let me just focus on one.

*❤ 16 · 🔁 0*

## 6/19

7/ So one big reason has to do with the fact that many countries subsidizes the costs associated with low density housing. Therefore one of our big policy goals is to ensure that we stop subsidizing low density housing.

*❤ 20 · 🔁 0*

## 7/19

8/ So in essence we don't necessarily hate single family detached suburbs, rather we want to ensure that those who decide to live in low density suburbs pay the full costs of such decision. Almost every country primarily relies on property taxes to fund city budgets.

*❤ 25 · 🔁 0*

## 8/19

9/ 80%+ of property tax revenues are based on the market value of the property, property taxes aren’t calculated based on the cost the housing type incurs the city in basic infrastructure and servicing needs.

*❤ 21 · 🔁 0*

## 9/19

10/ This means urban residents end up paying disproportionately more taxes than suburban residents as a function of the cost they incur to the city.

*❤ 23 · 🔁 0*

## 10/19

11/ Functionally this means that the market price of low density housing becomes artificially low while higher density urban houses become artificially high.

*❤ 18 · 🔁 0*

## 11/19

12/ There’s a myriad of policy prescriptions you can use to level out the playing field to ensure everyone pays the true costs associated with their housing decisions.

*❤ 18 · 🔁 0*

## 12/19

13/ You can reform the tax system to account for per property costs incurred on the city, or replace property taxes with a land value tax. You can upzone the whole city, like Japan does.

*❤ 19 · 🔁 0*

## 13/19

14/ You can make car routes more circuitous and ensure public transit and active transportation routes are as direct as possible to discourage driving (decreasing the the relative appeal of car dependent housing), like in The Netherlands.

## 14/19

15/ You can directly subsidize high density housing via public housing initiatives, like Vienna (making low density housing relatively more costly). You can have cities take a more active role in planning rather than a passive role via zoning.
